SIB × YSP International Youth Essay Competition 2026
Across Borders, Unequal Futures

Version: 5 November 2026

01 / Competition Rules

Competition Rules.

1. Organisers

The competition is organised by Social Issues Bubble (SIB) in collaboration with Youth for Sustainable Policy (YSP).

Proposed responsibilities:

  • SIB manages submissions, entrant communications, judging coordination, results and editorial preparation.
  • YSP supports outreach and competition promotion.
  • Designated representatives from both organisations agree the final results following the judging process.

2. Dates

All times are Singapore time (UTC+8).

  • Entries open: 1 December 2026, 12:00 am.
  • Entries close: 31 December 2026, 11:59 pm.
  • Results are scheduled for 15 January 2027.

Entries must be received through the official submission form before closing. Starting an upload before the deadline does not constitute submission.

Individual late entries are not accepted. If a verified failure of the official submission system materially prevents entry, the organisers may announce a general extension applicable equally to affected entrants.

3. Eligibility

  • Entrants must be aged 16–30 on 1 December 2026.
  • Entry is free.
  • Entries are welcome internationally, subject to applicable legal restrictions.
  • No nationality is excluded solely as an organisational policy. Any necessary legal restriction will be assessed for the relevant activity and explained where possible.
  • Organisers, judges and anyone with access to submissions or confidential judging materials through competition administration may not enter.
  • A close personal, family, supervisory or financial relationship with a judge must be disclosed. That judge will not assess the entry; if a fair alternative cannot be arranged, the organisers will explain the resulting eligibility decision.

Proposed under-18 policy: Entrants aged 16 or 17 must have permission from a parent or legal guardian. The form will ask them to confirm this. The organisers may request proportionate verification and will obtain any further consent needed before publication.

Do not submit identity documents unless the organisers specifically request them through a verified, secure process.

4. Entry requirements

Each entrant must:

  • Submit one individually authored essay responding to one of the six published prompts.
  • Write in English.
  • Submit 1,000–1,500 words.
  • Include citations and a reference list using a consistent style.
  • Submit original work that has not previously been publicly published.
  • Submit through the official Google Form as a PDF or DOCX file.

The word limit excludes the title, source citations and reference list. Headings, quotations and substantive footnotes count. Citations or notes must not be used to conceal additional argument.

Remove your name, school, contact details and other identifying information from the essay, filename and document properties where possible. Put entrant details in the form.

Private classroom submission or private feedback does not count as public publication. Entrants must disclose any previous public availability or overlapping competition submission for an eligibility decision.

Entrants should avoid publicly publishing or entering the essay elsewhere until results are announced or their entry is withdrawn.

5. Sources, feedback and AI tools

Entrants are responsible for the accuracy of their claims, quotations and references.

Basic spelling and grammar assistance is permitted, including tools that flag errors. Entrants must develop their own argument, analysis and prose.

AI-generated essays, paragraphs, substantive rewrites and translations are not permitted. Fabricated sources, quotations or evidence are prohibited.

Disclose permitted AI assistance in the submission form, naming the tool and describing its use. Select “None” if no such tool was used.

General feedback from a teacher, mentor or peer is permitted. Another person must not write or substantially rewrite the essay.

The organisers may request clarification about sources or the writing process. Integrity concerns receive human review. Automated AI-detector scores alone will not determine disqualification.

6. Submission changes and withdrawal

Entrants should check their file before submitting.

Before the deadline, an entrant may replace an essay using the same email address and clearly identify it as a replacement. Only the latest complete submission received before closing will be assessed.

After closing, substantive revisions are not permitted. The organisers may request an accessible copy of an unreadable file where its timely receipt can be verified; its content must remain unchanged.

Entrants may withdraw by contacting the competition team from their submission email address.

7. Judging

Eligible essays are assessed using:

CriterionWeight
Argument and critical analysis30%
Evidence and source use25%
Originality and insight20%
Practical implications15%
Clarity and structure10%

Proposed process:

  • Administrative checks occur before judging.
  • Essays are identified by entry number.
  • Judges do not receive entrant names, schools or nationality information.
  • Shortlisted essays receive at least two independent assessments.
  • Judges declare conflicts and recuse themselves where necessary.
  • Material scoring differences receive moderation.

Ties are resolved first by argument and critical analysis, then evidence and source use. If a tie remains, a non-conflicted additional reviewer assesses the tied essays.

The judging panel’s evaluation of literary and analytical merit is final. Entrants may report an administrative error, undisclosed conflict or procedural concern within seven calendar days of the results announcement. Such a review does not guarantee a different result.

Individual feedback is not guaranteed.

8. Recognition

The competition offers:

  • First, second and third place.
  • Up to five honourable mentions.
  • Digital award certificates for recognised entrants.
  • Consideration of selected essays for publication, subject to author agreement and editorial review.

No cash prize is offered.

Certificates will be issued jointly in SIB’s and YSP’s names and emailed to recognised entrants within 30 calendar days of the results announcement.

The organisers may withhold an award where no eligible entry meets the required standard, with an explanation accompanying the results.

[Confirm joint certificate approval and delivery capacity.]

9. Copyright and publication

Entrants retain copyright.

Submitting an essay permits the organisers to store it and share it privately with authorised administrators and judges for competition administration. It does not grant public publication rights.

Selected authors will receive a separate publication agreement covering:

  • Non-exclusive permission to publish on SIB’s website.
  • Any agreed publication on YSP’s channels.
  • Author credit and public profile preferences.
  • Approved promotional excerpts and links.
  • Editorial changes and author approval.
  • Publication duration and requests for removal.

Declining publication does not, by itself, remove an award.

10. Disqualification and procedural concerns

An entry may be disqualified for ineligibility, plagiarism, prohibited assistance, materially false declarations or failure to meet the submission requirements.

Before disqualification for an integrity concern, the entrant will ordinarily receive a summary of the concern and a reasonable opportunity to respond. Decisions will be reviewed by someone without a relevant conflict.

A minor administrative error may be corrected where this does not change the essay or create an unfair advantage.

11. Changes, postponement or cancellation

The organisers may make proportionate changes where necessary to address a technical failure, legal requirement or circumstance that prevents fair administration.

Material changes will be dated on the competition page and emailed to entrants already registered. Changes will not retrospectively alter judging criteria to favour particular entries.

If results must be delayed, the organisers will explain the reason and announce a revised date. If the competition is cancelled, entrants will be informed how their information and essays will be handled.

02 / Competition Privacy Notice

Competition Privacy Notice.

1. Who is responsible?

The competition’s responsible organisation or person is:

Kim Mei Tong
Social Issues Bubble
Privacy contact: socialissuesbubble@gmail.com

SIB administers the competition. YSP’s access to identifiable entrant information is limited to the responsibilities disclosed here.

[Confirm ownership of the form and storage accounts, and the actual division of access.]

2. Information collected

The proposed submission form collects:

  • Name and preferred public author name.
  • Email address.
  • Age on 1 December 2026 and eligibility confirmation.
  • Country or territory of residence.
  • Selected prompt, essay title and word count.
  • Essay file and references.
  • Assistance and originality declarations.
  • Under-18 permission confirmation, where applicable.
  • Optional referral source.
  • Separate optional newsletter preferences.

We may also keep relevant correspondence, submission timestamps, judging records and proportionate eligibility or permission verification.

We do not routinely request passport copies, identity numbers or home addresses. Please avoid including unnecessary sensitive information about yourself or others in your essay.

3. Why information is used

Information is used to:

  • Verify eligibility and administer submissions.
  • Arrange judging and address conflicts.
  • Investigate integrity or procedural concerns.
  • Communicate results and issue certificates.
  • Arrange agreed publication and attribution.
  • Maintain necessary competition records.

Newsletter messages require a separate voluntary choice. Entry does not subscribe you automatically to SIB or YSP updates.

4. Who can access it?

Access is limited according to role:

  • Designated administrators access entrant details and submissions.
  • Judges receive anonymised essays and scoring materials.
  • Publication editors receive selected essays and relevant contact details when needed.
  • Providers process information as required to supply the submission, storage and communications services.

YSP’s wider team and ambassador network do not receive the applicant database.

5. Service providers and overseas processing

The proposed process uses Google Forms and Google Drive, together with the organisers’ email services.

Information may be processed outside Singapore depending on provider arrangements and authorised reviewer locations. The organisers must confirm appropriate arrangements before providing overseas access.

6. Retention

Proposed schedule:

  • Non-selected essays, routine application records and routine correspondence: delete or anonymise within six months after results.
  • Identifiable judging and integrity records: retain for up to 12 months after results, unless a specific unresolved matter requires longer retention.
  • Award records: retain a minimal record of author name, essay title, award and year for verification.
  • Published essays and agreed public profiles: retain under the separate publication agreement.
  • Newsletter records: manage separately under the relevant subscription notice.

Any longer retention must have a documented purpose. Working copies and provider deletion processes must be included in the deletion plan.

[Adopt this schedule only after confirming operational capability.]

7. Public results and publication choices

Before announcing an identifiable award, the organisers will confirm the author’s public credit.

The proposed results listing includes the agreed author name, essay title and award. Country, school, photograph, biography and essay publication are optional and require separate agreement.

Entrants may request a pen name or discuss privacy concerns before publication. Where practicable, an award can be announced without identifying the recipient.

8. Requests and withdrawal

Contact [PRIVACY EMAIL] to request access or correction, withdraw consent, or raise a concern about retention or publication.

Requests will be handled under applicable law. We may seek proportionate identity verification.

If withdrawing consent prevents us from administering your entry, we will explain the consequences before taking the necessary action. Some information may need to remain where there is a continuing lawful purpose.

Withdrawing newsletter consent does not affect competition eligibility.

9. Updates

Material changes to this notice will be dated on the website and communicated where appropriate.

03 / Competition Enquiries

Competition Enquiries.

Questions about eligibility, prompts, submissions or results:

socialissuesbubble@gmail.com

Suggested service standard:
We aim to respond within three working days. A working day means Monday–Friday, excluding Singapore public holidays. This is a response target, not a guarantee.

Include your entry number, if available, and contact us from your submission email address. Do not send identity documents or sensitive personal information in your first message.

Technical questions should be raised as early as possible. Contacting the team does not extend the submission deadline.
