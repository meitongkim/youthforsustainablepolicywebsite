What Is YSP Learns?

Learn From Young People Changing Policy.

YSP Learns is where young people learn from each other how policy works and how to change it. Everything is made by YSP members, mentors and competition winners: routes, talks, guides and published work, all free.

Change rarely happens in a straight line, so there is more than one way in. Read a winning brief, listen to a conversation, follow a route, or join a programme and learn by doing.

  • Free, alwaysNo fees, no paywalls, no prerequisites.
  • Learn your wayRead, listen, practise or join a programme.
  • Publish and connectPut your work out under your own name and meet a global network.

Learning Routes

Six Routes, One Way In.

Each route is a path through one kind of policy work, with the skills it needs and the kind of work you will produce. Start with the one closest to what you care about.

Route 01 · Research

Build the rigour policy needs.

Original research, literature reviews, and evidence-based analysis — from primary sources to publishable briefs that hold up under citation.

Explore the Research route → Route 02 · Policy

Write policy people actually read.

Briefs, explainers, and submissions that move from idea to credible recommendation — and that committee staff, journalists, and MPs can use.

Explore the Policy route → Route 03 · Business

Where firms meet the public.

Feasibility, stakeholder maps, ESG, and corporate governance — the policy work that happens at the boundary of a firm and the public it answers to.

Explore the Business route → Route 04 · Diplomacy

Across borders, on record.

Multilateral institutions, position papers, and ASEAN-facing Track-II dialogue — under the supervision of senior practitioners.

Explore the Diplomacy route → Route 05 · Environment

Policy that meets the planet.

Climate adaptation, green finance, and sustainable urban policy — grounded in regional realities and the cost of acting late.

Explore the Environment route → Route 06 · Community

Lived experience, on the record.

Grassroots research, ethical storytelling, and campaign design — turning local knowledge into formal policy proposals.
