About The Collaboration

Three Questions. One Conversation.

Mind the Gap connects three questions: What are young people experiencing? What helps explain it? What could institutions do differently?

It gives contributors a supported route from an initial idea to published work, and creates a shared platform for BrainInk’s psychology and storytelling work and YSP’s research, policy analysis and public engagement.

The pilot programme brings together one digital publication, one companion policy brief and one public launch dialogue, developed over ten weeks. Each pilot edition is reviewed for participation, editorial quality and audience response, shaping the editions that follow.

Editorial Approach

Every Issue, Three Strands.

Strand 01

The Experience

How young people encounter the issue in everyday life, through personal essays, interviews, reported features and visual narratives.

Strand 02

The Explanation

The psychological concepts and research that help readers understand it, through research explainers, evidence reviews and illustrated articles.

Strand 03

The Response

How institutions could respond and what those responses would involve, through policy commentaries, proposal features and practitioner conversations.

Individual pieces need not cover all three strands. The editorial team connects them across the issue.

Issue 01 · Under Pressure

Achievement, Stress And Self-Worth.

How do our ideas and systems of achievement shape young people’s wellbeing, and what would meaningful support look like?

“Under Pressure” offers a focused starting point with room for different experiences: ambition, comparison, uncertainty, family expectations, assessment, transitions and access to support. Pressure is experienced differently across educational pathways and personal circumstances, so no single account will be presented as representative of all students.

The Pilot Programme

A Publication, A Brief And A Dialogue.

01 · The Digital Issue

Under Pressure

An accessible digital publication of 24–36 pages, released as a searchable PDF.

02 · The Companion Brief

BrainInk × YSP Brief

A two- to four-page brief setting out the main questions and findings, three to five supported options for institutional action, who could respond, and the limitations and open questions.

03 · The Launch Dialogue

A Public Conversation

A 60–75-minute moderated event, online or in person, with short contributions from authors, a discussion with invited speakers and audience questions, including an anonymous route for submitting them.

Who Leads What

Two Organisations, One Editorial Standard.

BrainInk Magazine

Psychology & Storytelling

Leads the editorial voice, personal essay, interview and psychology-explainer editing, checks that psychological concepts are explained accurately, and coordinates design, illustration and production.

Youth for Sustainable Policy

Policy & Dialogue

Helps contributors identify relevant institutions and decision-makers, edits policy analysis for specificity and feasibility, checks claims about policies, drafts the companion brief and coordinates the launch dialogue.

Together

Shared Standards

Both organisations agree the theme and schedule, select contributors, maintain a shared source record, manage contributor permissions and review the final materials and pilot together.

Contribute

Your Idea, Supported To Publication.

No previous publication experience is required.

The pilot programme is open to youth contributors aged 16–25, writing individually or in pairs. Visual contributions are welcome. Researchers, educators and practitioners may be invited to contribute interviews or commentary. Contributors will be recruited through an open call for pitches alongside targeted invitations.

The open call

What To Pitch

  • A working title and preferred format
  • A 150–250-word pitch explaining your central question and approach
  • The perspective or experience you would bring
  • Two or three starting sources for research-led pieces
  • Any proposed interviews or visual elements
  • Your availability during the production period
  • Any editorial or accessibility support you would find useful

A writing sample is optional, and never a barrier for first-time contributors.

How Pieces Are Edited

From Pitch To Proof.

  1. 01Commission

    Agree the angle, length, editor and deadline.

  2. 02First Draft

    Develop the argument, narrative and evidence.

  3. 03Substantive Edit

    Address structure, clarity, missing context and unsupported claims.

  4. 04Revision

    Resolve editorial questions and supply missing sources.

  5. 05Fact-Check & Copy-Edit

    Verify quotations, references, terminology and factual details.

  6. 06Proof Approval

    Review the final text and layout before publication.

Pieces that cross psychology and policy receive input from both organisations. Plan for one substantive revision and one final checking round, and published errors will be corrected transparently.

Contributor Guidelines

Care, Evidence And Consent.

Writing & Evidence

For A Curious Reader

Explain specialist terms and distinguish personal experience, interpretation and research findings. Research-led pieces use credible, traceable sources, represent limitations and avoid presenting associations as proof of causation.

Personal Stories

Your Choice, Always

You choose how much of your own experience to share. Personal disclosure is never a condition of participation. Interviews need consent for recording and publication, agreed attribution or anonymity, and a chance to verify quotations.

Originality & Rights

Your Work Stays Yours

Authors and artists retain ownership. Third-party material must be credited and cleared. AI tools may support your work, but material assistance is disclosed to editors, and fabricated quotations, sources or evidence are never acceptable.

The Pilot Programme

Ten Weeks, Idea To Launch.

  1. Week 1Confirm scope, roles and editorial brief
  2. Week 2Launch the open call and invite contributors
  3. Week 3Select contributions and hold an orientation
  4. Week 4Develop outlines, sources and interview plans
  5. Week 5First drafts and substantive editing begin
  6. Week 6Edits returned; companion brief outlined
  7. Week 7Revised drafts; evidence and quotations checked
  8. Week 8Copy-editing, layout and the companion brief
  9. Week 9Contributor approvals and accessibility checks
  10. Week 10Publish the issue and brief; launch dialogue

Fact-checking and contributor approval are never compressed: if drafts run late, the publication date moves instead.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions.

When does the open call open?+

The open call for Issue 01 opens soon. Register your interest and we’ll let you know as soon as pitches open.

What support will I get?+

Every contributor works with a named editor, receives substantive feedback, and approves their final text and layout before publication, with full publication credit.

Do I have to write about my own experience?+

No. Personal disclosure is never a condition of participation. Research explainers, reported features, policy commentaries, interviews and visual work are all welcome.

Who owns my work?+

You do. The publication agreement gives BrainInk and YSP permission to publish the agreed work in the issue and use attributed excerpts for promotion. Any other reuse, including substantial adaptation into the companion brief, is agreed with you.

Do I need to run a survey or original research?+

No. Contributions do not require original surveys or psychological research. Any proposed data collection should be discussed with editors before it begins.
