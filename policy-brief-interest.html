Welcome

Youth for Sustainable Policy (YSP) is a global youth-led initiative dedicated to empowering the next generation to engage critically with sustainability, governance, and public policy.

The YSP Global Policy Brief Competition is our flagship global writing competition, inviting youth to engage critically with climate and energy policy.

Participants will produce a concise, research-backed policy brief evaluating real-world policy and proposing actionable improvements.

This competition is aligned with YSP’s Research & Policy and Environment tracks, and serves as a pathway for publication, recognition, and advancement within YSP.

Competition Information

Participants will submit a 600–900 word policy brief responding to a climate and energy policy prompt.

Submissions will be evaluated based on

  • Argument quality
  • Evidence use
  • Accessibility
  • Policy practicality

Objectives

  • Develop research synthesis and policy argumentation skills
  • Introduce YSP’s publication and writing standards
  • Produce accessible, high-quality climate policy writing
  • Identify high-potential contributors for fast-tracked opportunities

Eligibility

Participants must meet one of the following:

  • Aged 16–30
  • Recent graduates welcome, from any field of study
  • Finished middle school, at minimum

No prior policy writing experience is required.

Winning Categories

  • 01: Junior (Secondary and high school entrants)
  • 02: Open (University students, graduates and young professionals)

Important Notes

  • One submission per participant
  • Work must be original
  • Full template and style guide will be provided

Competition Prompt

The 2027 prompt will be announced when the competition opens. For reference, this was the 2026 prompt:

2026 promptChoose one fossil fuel subsidy reform policy implemented in any country in the last five years. Evaluate its effectiveness and recommend one improvement a national government could adopt by 2030.

Guidelines

Your policy brief should:

  • Clearly identify the selected policy
  • Evaluate its outcomes using evidence
  • Analyse limitations or gaps
  • Propose one realistic, well-justified improvement
