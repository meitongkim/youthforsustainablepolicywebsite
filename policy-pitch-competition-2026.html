01 / About

Make The Case For Change.

This competition builds directly on the Global Policy Brief Competition, but creates a different experience.

Instead of writing a conventional policy brief, participants must identify a real problem, propose a feasible intervention, and persuade a judging panel that their solution deserves serious consideration.

The competition can also become an entry point into the planned YSP Global Policy Lab and Civic Innovation Hub.

03 / Structure

Two Rounds. One Proposal.

Round 1

Open Submissions

Participants submit a five-slide policy pitch deck and a concise written summary.

Round 2

Finalist Pitch

Finalists deliver a four-minute pitch followed by a moderated question-and-answer session.

05 / Themes

Six Policy Areas. Plus One Open.

Propose a solution in one of six policy areas, or in the interdisciplinary category.

  1. 01

    Climate & Environmental Policy

    Climate adaptation, energy, waste, water, biodiversity and circular economy

  2. 02

    Education & Youth Development

    Access to education, skills, youth employment and civic participation

  3. 03

    Economic & Social Policy

    Inequality, affordable living, labour, inclusion and sustainable growth

  4. 04

    Technology & Digital Governance

    AI governance, digital accessibility, privacy and responsible innovation

  5. 05

    Public Health & Community Wellbeing

    Healthcare access, prevention, environmental health and community care

  6. 06

    Governance & International Cooperation

    Public accountability, institutions, international development and policy coordination

  7. 07

    Open / Interdisciplinary

    Evidence-based ideas connecting multiple public policy fields

The strongest pitches address a specific jurisdiction or community and demonstrate an understanding of implementation constraints. Proposals are evaluated for their evidence and feasibility, not their political alignment.

06 / Pitch Deck

Five Slides. One Clear Case.

Required five-slide pitch deck.

  1. YSP POLICY PITCH01

    The Problem

    What is the policy problem, who is affected, and why does it require action?

  2. YSP POLICY PITCH02

    The Evidence

    What do reliable sources and existing policies tell us about the issue?

  3. YSP POLICY PITCH03

    The Policy Proposal

    What specific intervention are you recommending, and who would implement it?

  4. YSP POLICY PITCH04

    Implementation

    What are the steps, stakeholders, resources, costs and foreseeable challenges?

  5. YSP POLICY PITCH05

    Impact & Evaluation

    What outcomes are expected, how would success be measured, and what limitations remain?

Illustrative slide structure. A title slide and references page may be added without counting toward the five content slides.

A Transparent 100-Point Rubric.

Written proposal and pitch deck · 100 pts

    15

  2. Evidence quality & policy understanding

    20

  3. Originality & substantive contribution

    15

  4. Feasibility & implementation

    25

  5. Expected impact & evaluation

    15

  6. Clarity & accessibility

    10

Finals

Live or recorded pitch · 100 pts

  1. Policy substance

    30

  2. Feasibility and implementation

    25

  3. Expected impact

    20

  4. Clarity of presentation

    15

  5. Response to questions

    10

Recorded pitches

A submitted recording is evaluated on the same substantive criteria as a live pitch, with an equivalent written-question option.

Fair review

Judges should declare conflicts of interest. Where possible, initial screening should be anonymised, use at least two independent reviewers per entry, and have a documented process for resolving large scoring differences.

09 / Rules

Competition Rules.

YSP Global Policy Pitch Competition 2026 · Draft for organiser approval.

1.Organiser

The YSP Global Policy Pitch Competition 2026 is organised by Youth for Sustainable Policy (YSP).

2.Purpose

The competition invites young people to develop evidence-based, practical public policy proposals addressing social, environmental, economic, governance, or technological challenges.

3.Important dates
  • Opens: 15 November 2026, 00:00 SGT (UTC+8)
  • Closes: 10 January 2027, 23:59 SGT (UTC+8)
  • Finalists announced: 24 January 2027
  • Online finals: 7 February 2027
  • Results announced: 14 February 2027

These dates are provisional until formally approved and published by YSP.

4.Eligibility

Proposed: Open internationally to participants aged 16–30 on 15 November 2026, subject to applicable legal restrictions.

Participants may enter individually or in teams of two or three. All team members must satisfy the eligibility requirements.

Entrants under 18 may be required to provide parent or guardian consent before participating in finalist events or having identifiable information published.

5.Entry fee

Participation is free.

6.Submission requirements

Each entry must include:

  1. One PDF policy pitch deck, containing no more than five content slides.
  2. A written policy summary of 250–400 words.
  3. References supporting key factual claims and any externally sourced content.
  4. The designated submission form and required declarations.

A separate title slide and reference slide are permitted.

Participants must identify a specific policy problem, present a proposed intervention, explain implementation considerations, and describe expected outcomes.

7.Originality, sources and AI use

Entries must be original work produced by the declared participants.

Generative AI tools may be used for limited assistance with research organisation, language editing, or visual design, provided such use is disclosed.

Entrants remain responsible for factual accuracy, verification of references, originality of analysis, and the final submission.

Fabricated sources, plagiarism, undisclosed substantive AI-generated work, and misrepresentation of evidence may result in disqualification following review.

8.Assessment

Entries will be assessed using the published criteria for problem definition, evidence, originality, feasibility, impact and clarity.

Finalists will be invited to present their proposals to a judging panel. Reasonable remote and asynchronous participation alternatives will be provided where possible.

9.Conflicts of interest

Organisers, judges, and individuals directly involved in competition administration may not evaluate entries in which they have a personal, supervisory, financial, or other material conflict of interest.

Any further entrant eligibility exclusions must be approved and published before submissions open.

10.Intellectual property

Participants retain ownership of their original work.

Submission does not automatically transfer copyright to YSP.

YSP will obtain separate permission before publishing, adapting, or promoting entrants' full proposals beyond what is necessary to administer and announce the competition.

11.Recognition

Finalists and winners may receive digital recognition, certificates, and invitations to optional contributor profiles.

Any prizes, financial awards, mentoring benefits, or publication opportunities must be confirmed before being advertised.

12.Privacy

Participant information will be used for competition administration, assessment and necessary communications, in accordance with a published privacy notice.

Public display of portraits, biographies, professional links, and extended profiles requires appropriate consent.

13.Changes and enquiries

Any material changes to competition rules or dates will be communicated transparently to participants.

The final published rules must identify an official enquiry contact, submission method, privacy notice and procedure for questions or complaints.
